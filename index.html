The Work

In elementary school art classes, I was never the kid who could paint or draw very well. I always thought artistic ability would come with age, but it never really did for me. What I eventually realized is that my version of creativity just looked different.

Give me a messy dataset, inconsistent reporting, or a process held together by spreadsheets, and that’s usually where I do my best work. That’s what pulled me toward data analytics in the first place.

My work today focuses on SQL, Python, ETL workflows, dashboard design, and automation. Sometimes that means turning a process that once took days into something that runs in seconds. Other times it means rebuilding decades of legacy data into something teams can finally rely on.

Most recently, I worked on a national-scale CRM migration project in the non-profit sector, supporting reporting that influences million-dollar campaign strategy, and large-scale data cleanup efforts.

The work became so central to the migration that I was later brought on as a contractor to support a much larger rollout across United Ways all throughout Canada.

Off the Screen

I’ve always believed data can be especially powerful in the non-profit space, which is what led me to get involved in Data for Good datathons. Through those projects, I’ve worked with organizations on challenges ranging from improving volunteer engagement at the city level to helping youth-focused charities better understand their workshop and survey data.

I also coach a competitive youth basketball team that travels across Ontario, and help run introductory basketball clinics in collaboration with MLSE. A big part of that work is creating an environment where kids can build confidence, discipline, and teamwork alongside the sport itself.

When I’m not buried in a SQL query or on the basketball court, you’ll probably find me collecting fragrances, hitting the gym, hiking local trails, or dealing with the stress of being a Lakers fan.
